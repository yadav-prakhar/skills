# Life domains and cross-domain map

Use this file to identify which parts of life are materially involved in a question. It is a map, not a mandatory checklist. Do not force every domain into every answer.

## 1. Relationships and marriage

**Includes:** partnership, dating, marriage preparation, shared responsibilities, communication, conflict, trust, intimacy, affection, expectations, boundaries, money conversations, and major joint decisions.

**Useful questions:**
- What do I want the relationship to feel like in practice?
- What is the other person likely experiencing, based on evidence rather than mind-reading?
- Is the disagreement about the immediate issue, or about a recurring unmet need or expectation?
- What would repair look like? What would a fair boundary look like?
- Am I trying to be understood, to understand, to win, to avoid discomfort, or to make a decision?

**Watch for:** scorekeeping, contempt, stonewalling, repeated broken agreements, coercion, unequal emotional labor, and conflicts that cannot be discussed safely. Do not label a relationship from a single anecdote.

## 2. Family and responsibilities

**Includes:** parents, siblings, extended family, caregiving, household roles, family finances, traditions, and competing obligations.

**Useful questions:**
- Which responsibilities are freely chosen, reciprocal, urgent, or genuinely mine?
- Which expectations are explicit and which are assumed?
- What can I contribute without creating resentment or harming my own household?
- Is guilt driving this decision more than care or considered duty?
- What boundary can be communicated respectfully and consistently?

**Watch for:** false binaries between loyalty and independence, triangulation, financial dependence without clarity, and using family harmony to silence legitimate needs.

## 3. Mental and emotional well-being

**Includes:** emotional awareness, stress, grief, confidence, anger, shame, anxiety, loneliness, resilience, identity, and patterns of coping.

**Useful questions:**
- What emotion is present, and what might it be signaling?
- What happened immediately before the reaction?
- What story am I telling myself, and what evidence supports or challenges it?
- What do I need right now: safety, rest, connection, expression, perspective, or action?
- Is this a recurring or escalating problem that deserves professional support?

**Watch for:** shame spirals, avoidance, rumination, self-attack, numbing, and treating feelings as proof. Avoid diagnosis.

## 4. Physical health and fitness

**Includes:** movement, strength, cardiovascular fitness, mobility, nutrition, sleep, recovery, preventive care, and sustainable routines.

**Useful questions:**
- What is the goal: health, strength, energy, performance, confidence, or enjoyment?
- What is a safe baseline given current health, ability, and injury history?
- Is the routine sustainable alongside work, family, and sleep?
- What should be measured, and what does not need tracking?
- Does this require a clinician, physiotherapist, dietitian, or other qualified professional?

**Watch for:** pain being ignored, extreme restriction, overtraining, supplement claims, and optimizing a metric at the expense of health.

## 5. Personal finance

**Includes:** cash flow, budgets, emergency funds, debt, insurance, investments, taxes, credit, housing, major purchases, and financial independence.

**Useful questions:**
- What is the goal and when will the money be needed?
- What is the downside, and can I absorb it?
- How do fees, taxes, liquidity, inflation, and opportunity cost change the comparison?
- Is this a need, a durable preference, an impulse, or a status purchase?
- What current information or professional advice is necessary?

**Watch for:** return chasing, concentration, hidden leverage, lifestyle inflation, underinsurance, and confusing affordability with value.

## 6. Social life and friendships

**Includes:** friendship, community, social confidence, belonging, shared interests, and maintaining meaningful connections.

**Useful questions:**
- Which relationships are mutual and nourishing?
- Who do I want to make more time for?
- Am I withdrawing because I need rest, because of conflict, or because of fear?
- What small invitation or act of follow-through would strengthen connection?

**Watch for:** social comparison, keeping score, confusing popularity with connection, and letting important relationships survive only on good intentions.

## 7. Identity and character

**Includes:** values, integrity, courage, discipline, self-respect, learning, communication, and personal standards.

**Useful questions:**
- What value is at stake?
- What would I do if I were not trying to impress, appease, or avoid anyone?
- What behavior would make me respect myself afterward?
- Am I holding myself to a standard I would consider fair for another person?
- What specific action would demonstrate the quality I want to build?

**Watch for:** perfectionism, performative self-improvement, rigid self-labels, and confusing one failure with a fixed identity.

## 8. Lifestyle and experiences

**Includes:** time use, home, travel, hobbies, creativity, recreation, routines, consumption, and work-life boundaries.

**Useful questions:**
- What would make an ordinary week feel more humane and enjoyable?
- What experience am I postponing without a good reason?
- Is convenience worth the cost here?
- Does this plan leave room for rest and unstructured time?
- What am I buying, and what am I hoping the purchase will make me feel?

**Watch for:** turning leisure into another achievement, overplanning, and postponing enjoyment until an arbitrary milestone.

## 9. Purpose and meaning

**Includes:** contribution, spirituality or philosophy when relevant, legacy, service, meaning, and long-term direction.

**Useful questions:**
- What feels meaningful even when it is difficult or invisible?
- Which values do I want my calendar and choices to reflect?
- What do I want to contribute to people beyond myself?
- Which ambitions are mine, and which are borrowed expectations?
- What would a good life look like in ordinary daily terms?

**Watch for:** expecting one grand purpose to solve every problem, comparing life timelines, and confusing prestige with meaning.

## Cross-domain interactions

Use only the connections that actually matter:

| Decision area | Common ripple effects |
|---|---|
| Marriage or relationship decision | Family expectations, finances, living arrangements, emotional well-being, time, and identity |
| Relocation or career change | Partnership, family responsibilities, friendships, finances, health routines, and belonging |
| Major purchase | Cash reserves, debt, future flexibility, stress, shared goals, and opportunity cost |
| Fitness or diet change | Health, sleep, injury risk, time, social life, and self-image |
| Family obligation | Partnership, finances, time, autonomy, and resentment or connection |
| New long-term goal | Existing commitments, rest, relationships, identity, and enjoyment |

## Practical rule

Name the two or three domains most affected. Mention a wider ripple only if it could change the decision. A good whole-life answer is selective, not exhaustive.
