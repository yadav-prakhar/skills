# Difficult conversation preparation

Use this to prepare for a conversation that matters. The purpose is clarity and mutual understanding, not scripting every response or controlling the other person's reaction.

## 3. My part

- What might I need to own or acknowledge?
- Am I entering the conversation to understand, or to win?
- What assumptions should I check rather than state as facts?

## 4. The message

Use a clear, specific, non-accusatory structure:

> "When [specific event] happened, I felt [emotion] because [impact or need]. I would like [specific request]. How did you experience it?"

**My draft:**

[Write the words I want to use]

## 5. Listen and respond

- What might the other person say that I should genuinely consider?
- What would change my view?
- What can I acknowledge without pretending to agree?
- What boundary or point must remain clear?

## 6. Timing and safety

- Is this a suitable time and setting?
- Are we calm enough to talk productively?
- Is there a power imbalance, threat, coercion, or risk of retaliation?
- Would a supported, mediated, or professional conversation be safer?

Do not use this template to prepare a confrontation when doing so could increase danger. Prioritize safety and independent support.

## 7. Desired next step

- What agreement, action, or clarity would be useful?
- When should we revisit it?
- What will I do if no agreement is reached?
