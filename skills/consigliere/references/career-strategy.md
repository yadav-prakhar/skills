# Career strategy, promotion, and professional reputation

Use when the user is considering growth, promotion, role changes, visibility, performance positioning, or a longer-term career move.

## 1. Define the desired outcome

Distinguish among:
- More scope or autonomy.
- Promotion or title change.
- Compensation improvement.
- Better manager, team, or working conditions.
- Specialist depth or leadership path.
- More meaningful work or stronger learning.
- Reduced risk, workload, or values conflict.
- An external move.

Do not assume promotion is always the best outcome.

## 2. Understand the decision system

Establish the formal criteria, review cycle, budget constraints, decision-makers, evidence required, and timing. Separate official criteria from informal norms, and do not treat unverified rumors as policy.

## 3. Build an evidence-backed case

A useful promotion or scope case connects:
- Responsibilities actually carried.
- Outcomes and measurable impact.
- Complexity, ambiguity, and judgment demonstrated.
- Influence beyond assigned tasks.
- Reliability and follow-through.
- Evidence of operating at the next level.
- What changed for customers, teammates, cost, risk, quality, or delivery.

Prefer concrete results and corroboration to adjectives. Make invisible work visible without exaggerating ownership or taking credit from collaborators.

## 4. Visibility without self-promotion theater

- Share concise progress and outcomes at useful decision points.
- Connect work to organizational priorities.
- Make dependencies and contributions legible.
- Credit collaborators explicitly.
- Give leaders decision-ready summaries rather than activity logs.
- Ask for feedback early enough to act on it.

Visibility should improve coordination and understanding, not merely increase message volume.

## 5. Sponsor, mentor, and manager relationships

A mentor offers guidance; a sponsor is willing to advocate or create access; a manager assesses and allocates work; these roles may overlap but are not interchangeable. Build trust through reliable work, clear aspirations, thoughtful asks, and mutual value. Do not treat a sponsor as a political insurance policy.

## 6. Identify gaps honestly

Ask what evidence is missing, which responsibilities are not yet demonstrated, what feedback is consistent, and what opportunity could close the gap. Distinguish a real skill or scope gap from opaque criteria or a blocked pathway.

## 7. Promotion conversation

A clear framing:

> "I want to understand what readiness for [target role/level] looks like here. Based on [specific outcomes and scope], where do you see me meeting that bar, and what evidence or experience is still missing? Can we agree on concrete opportunities and a date to review progress?"

Record agreed criteria and follow up professionally. If the criteria keep moving, assess whether the path is credible.

## 8. External options and exit timing

Compare the current role with realistic alternatives: learning, compensation, scope, manager quality, stability, location, values, and future optionality. Do not bluff with an offer or threaten resignation unless prepared to act. Preserve relationships and comply with contractual and security obligations.
