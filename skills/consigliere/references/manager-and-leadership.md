# Manager and leadership relationships

Use for manager alignment, executive communication, expectation setting, performance feedback, upward management, and leadership dynamics.

## 1. Understand what the manager is accountable for

Explore their goals, pressure, decision authority, dependencies, risk exposure, and communication preferences. Do not assume their motives from title or demeanor. A manager may be balancing competing priorities they cannot fully disclose.

## 2. Manage expectations explicitly

Clarify:
- Desired outcome and definition of done.
- Priority relative to competing work.
- Decision owner and approval path.
- Timeline, dependencies, and constraints.
- What trade-off is required if time or resources are insufficient.

A useful upward-management update is: context, current status, risk, options, recommendation, and decision needed.

## 3. Disagree without making it personal

A productive disagreement:
1. Confirm shared objective.
2. State the specific concern and evidence.
3. Explain the consequence or risk.
4. Offer an alternative or test.
5. Ask what would change the decision.

Do not confuse respectful dissent with endless debate. Once a legitimate decision is made, clarify ownership and boundaries; do not silently undermine execution. Raise serious safety, legal, ethical, or compliance concerns through appropriate channels.

## 4. Receiving difficult feedback

Separate content, delivery, and evidence. Ask for examples, impact, expectations, and what observable improvement would look like. Acknowledge valid points without accepting inaccurate claims. Document agreed actions neutrally.

## 5. Communicating to executives

Lead with the decision or implication. Include only the context needed to understand the choice. State options, recommendation, risk, owner, and date. Do not bury bad news, overstate confidence, or surprise leaders late when an earlier warning was possible.

## 6. When alignment is not possible

Try clarification, written recap, explicit trade-offs, or a bounded escalation. If priorities repeatedly conflict, ask the accountable leader to choose. Do not absorb impossible commitments and then frame predictable failure as a surprise.
