# Negotiation, influence, and stakeholder alignment

Use for compensation, scope, resources, priorities, cross-functional alignment, and persuasion.

## 1. Define interests and constraints

Clarify what each side may value, what is negotiable, what is constrained, who has authority, and what alternatives exist. Distinguish stated positions from underlying interests without assuming secret motives.

## 2. Establish your alternatives

Understand your best realistic alternative if no agreement is reached. Do not inflate it or bluff. Consider timing, dependencies, switching costs, relationships, and the value of waiting.

## 3. Prepare the ask

Define:
- Target outcome.
- Minimum acceptable outcome or walk-away condition, if applicable.
- Evidence supporting the request.
- Issues that can be traded.
- Questions that reveal constraints.
- What must be documented after agreement.

## 4. Influence through alignment

Connect the proposal to shared goals, evidence, risks, and a clear decision. Adapt the amount of context to the audience. Invite objections and test assumptions. Make it easy for stakeholders to support the proposal without misrepresenting their interests.

## 5. Make concessions deliberately

Trade rather than simply give. A concession should be proportionate, understandable, and preferably reciprocal. Do not concede something important merely to relieve conversational discomfort. Consider whether a package or staged agreement is better than a single number.

## 6. Compensation discussions

Use current, credible market data where relevant and distinguish base pay, variable pay, equity, benefits, level, location, and review timing. Do not present online salary anecdotes as definitive. Frame the discussion around role scope, demonstrated impact, and market context. Verify current local rules and tax implications when material.

## 7. Close with clarity

Summarize what was agreed, what remains open, owners, dates, conditions, and the next review. A friendly conversation is not necessarily a commitment; distinguish verbal encouragement from an approved decision.
