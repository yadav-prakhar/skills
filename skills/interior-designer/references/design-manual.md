# Interior design field manual

This consolidated reference is a practical decision framework, not a substitute for local codes, manufacturer data or site inspection.

## 1. Room intelligence and evidence

Inspect all supplied images separately before synthesizing them. Maintain an evidence ledger:
- `OBSERVED`: directly visible in a supplied view.
- `USER_STATED`: explicitly reported by the occupant.
- `MEASURED`: supplied measurement or verified site dimension.
- `INFERRED`: plausible interpretation, not confirmed.
- `PROPOSED`: new design intervention.
- `UNKNOWN`: unavailable or occluded.
- `VERIFY`: must be checked before purchase or work.

Record wall boundaries, openings, ceiling, beams, columns, niches, fixed joinery, existing furniture, lights, fans, switches, sockets and visible services. Note camera viewpoint, lens distortion, occlusions and missing views. A photograph can suggest dimensions but cannot establish precise dimensions. Never infer hidden wiring, circuit capacity, wall construction or structural suitability from appearance.

## 2. Space planning

Plan in this order: activities and users; fixed architecture and services; measured base plan; circulation; primary furniture; storage; secondary pieces; styling. Test the real movement envelope: doors, drawers, wardrobe shutters, chair pull-back, bed access, curtain travel, cleaning and maintenance. Use actual product dimensions where possible. Mark conceptual footprints as assumptions. Verify delivery access through stairs, lifts, corridors and turns. Do not treat generic clearance numbers as code; adapt to users, furniture, local standards and accessibility needs.

## 3. Lighting design

Audit existing lighting before proposing additions. Inventory every source with location, type, control, direction, likely purpose and known power access. Separate:
- Ambient: general room illumination.
- Task: reading, grooming, work, cooking or other focused activity.
- Accent: highlights architecture, art or texture.
- Decorative: visible fixture or atmosphere.

For each proposed source, specify purpose, exact placement, mounting surface, power route/source, switching/control, glare, maintenance and installation. Check shadows, reflected glare, colour appearance, dimming compatibility, heat and access for replacement. Do not pretend a plug point or ceiling junction exists if it is not confirmed. If a new wardrobe or wall panel hides an existing light, explicitly resolve the loss: relocate, replace, omit with user approval, or redesign the joinery. Do not use a lamp as a substitute for general illumination unless its output and placement actually serve that purpose.

Prefer layered control (separate circuits or independently switched layers) where feasible. For product comparisons, verify lumen output, beam angle, CCT, CRI, dimming, driver, IP rating where relevant, warranty and physical dimensions from manufacturer documentation. Kelvin alone does not determine comfort; brightness, distribution, glare and surface reflectance matter.

## 4. Colour and composition

Define palette roles rather than listing disconnected colours: dominant/base, secondary, accent, wood/metal and textile. Evaluate undertone, daylight direction, artificial-light CCT, adjacent materials, room size and maintenance. Test physical samples in the actual room at multiple times of day. Screens and generated renders are not reliable colour proofs. Use contrast for legibility and wayfinding; consider visual hierarchy, rhythm, proportion, negative space and focal points. Avoid repeating a feature everywhere merely to create cohesion.

## 5. Materials and finishes

Choose by use, moisture, heat, abrasion, cleaning chemicals, UV exposure, repairability, emissions/odour, edge treatment and local service. Ask for samples, technical datasheets, warranty and installation method. For stone, tile, laminate, veneer, paint, metal, glass, upholstery and flooring, verify substrate preparation and transitions. Consider slip resistance in wet areas and moisture detailing near plumbing. Do not infer a product's performance from appearance or marketing adjectives. Confirm exact finish, batch/shade, thickness, dimensions, edge profile and maintenance instructions before ordering.

## 6. Furniture and joinery

For every piece, record overall width/depth/height, internal usable dimensions, door/drawer swing, hardware, load, ventilation, cable access, service access, skirting/wall irregularities and installation tolerance. Wardrobes need usable hanging depth, shelf reach, shutter clearance and ventilation strategy. Check bed access and linen-changing space. Verify wall substrate and fixing design with a qualified installer; heavy cabinetry must not rely on assumed wall strength. For custom work, produce dimensioned elevations, section details, finish schedule, hardware schedule and written scope before fabrication.

## 7. Style and reference analysis

Treat styles as vocabularies, not costumes. Modern, contemporary, minimal, Scandinavian/Nordic, Japandi, mid-century, industrial, traditional, transitional, eclectic, rustic and regional Indian craft-informed directions can be mixed thoughtfully. When analyzing inspiration, identify transferable principles (palette, proportion, texture, silhouette, lighting, rhythm) separately from room-specific architecture and bespoke details. State what is intentionally excluded to avoid visual clutter. Use cultural references respectfully and specifically.

## 8. Visualization control and QA

Before image generation/editing, define:
1. Source photo and camera view.
2. Geometry lock: walls, openings, ceiling, floor, beams, columns, niches and perspective.
3. Existing-condition lock: every item to keep unchanged.
4. Explicit removal list, only if approved.
5. Addition list with exact location relative to fixed features.
6. Materials, colours and lighting.
7. Unknowns that must not be depicted as facts.

Use spatially specific instructions. Keep object scale plausible. Do not invent coves, extra outlets, windows, doors, fixtures, hidden corridors or architectural changes. After rendering, compare against source for geometry, camera, existing objects, additions/removals, scale, lighting plausibility and material consistency. Any major geometry or safety-critical mismatch invalidates it as a faithful visualization. Label output as a concept render, not a measured drawing or execution reference.

## 9. India procurement and project execution

Use metric and INR unless requested otherwise. Separate item price, delivery, taxes, installation, electrical/civil work and contingency. Check city-level stock, seller identity, model number, dimensions, warranty, service coverage, returns and installation requirements. Online prices are time-sensitive; date-stamp them and recheck before purchase. Climate advice must follow actual site exposure, not assumptions about all of India.

Prepare a scope of work, drawings, bill of quantities, fixture/material schedules, budget, dependencies, milestones, exclusions, payment stages, site-protection plan, testing and handover checklist. Get comparable written quotations. Confirm who supplies, installs, transports, disposes and warrants each item.

## 10. Safety and professional boundaries

Refer structural changes, wall chasing, heavy suspended loads, electrical circuits, earthing, wet-area waterproofing, gas, HVAC, fire systems and code/accessibility questions to qualified local professionals. Do not prescribe DIY work on live electrical systems or load-bearing structures. Confirm permissions in rented homes and apartment buildings. Never obstruct exits, ventilation, sprinklers, smoke detectors, access panels, electrical panels or maintenance clearances.
